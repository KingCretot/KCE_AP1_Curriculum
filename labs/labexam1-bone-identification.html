Lab Exam 1 Review: Bone Identification

Your course describes Lab Exam 1 as bone identification: you will look at whole bones, sectioned bone models, and diagrams of compact bone, then name the shape class, the region, or the microscopic structure being pointed to. This review walks through the long bone from the outside in, then zooms down to the osteon. If your Lab Exam 1 also covers Labs 1 to 3, work through those reviews as well.

What this lab tests you on

Name regions of a sectioned long bone (epiphysis, metaphysis, diaphysis)
Identify membranes, marrow, and bone types on a model

Bone Shapes

Classify by shape, not by size

Shape is about proportions. A long bone is longer than it is wide and has a shaft, so the finger bones count even though they are small. A short bone is cube-like. A flat bone is a thin plate. An irregular bone fits none of these. A sesamoid bone forms inside a tendon; the patella is the one you must know.

Naming varies by course. Your week 7 study guide lists four main shapes (long, short, flat, irregular). Many lab manuals add sesamoid as a fifth, and a few add sutural (Wormian) bones, the small extra bones in skull sutures. If you are asked for the shape of the patella, "sesamoid" is the expected answer; "short" is accepted only in courses that use four classes.

Long Bone Anatomy

The sectioned long bone, outside to inside

Read the model top to bottom, then outside to inside. Ends = epiphyses; shaft = diaphysis; the flared zone between = metaphysis. Outside: articular cartilage on the joint surfaces and periosteum everywhere else. Wall: compact bone. Inside the ends: spongy bone with red marrow. Inside the shaft: the medullary cavity with yellow marrow, lined by endosteum.

On a plastic model: the medullary cavity is usually painted yellow, red marrow areas red, articular cartilage blue or white, and the periosteum may be a peeled-back flap. Colors differ between models, so identify by position, not color.

Microscopic Anatomy

Compact bone, spongy bone, and the osteon

Compact bone is a bundle of osteons. Each osteon is a set of concentric lamellae (rings) around a central canal. Osteocytes sit in lacunae between the rings and reach each other through canaliculi. Perforating canals cross at right angles to link central canals. Between osteons are interstitial lamellae; around the whole shaft are circumferential lamellae. Spongy bone has no osteons: it is a lattice of trabeculae with red marrow in the gaps.

Central vs. perforating canal, the fast way: in a cross section, central canals look like round holes in the middle of ring systems. Perforating canals look like channels that cut sideways across the rings, joining one central canal to another or to the surface. Central = lengthwise; perforating = crosswise.

Bone Cells

Four bone cells: make, build, maintain, break down

Bone cells on and in the bone matrix (schematic)
CellWhereJobHow to recognize it
Osteogenic (osteoprogenitor) cellPeriosteum and endosteum, on bone surfacesStem cell; divides and becomes osteoblastsFlat cell lying on the surface
OsteoblastGrowing or repairing bone surfacesSecretes osteoid (unmineralized matrix) and starts mineralizationCuboidal cells in a row; "b" for build
OsteocyteInside lacunaeMaintains the matrix; senses mechanical stressAn osteoblast that walled itself in
OsteoclastIn pits on bone surfacesDissolves matrix with acid and enzymes, releasing calcium (activated by PTH)Huge, multinucleated, ruffled border; "c" for chew/clear

Remodeling in one sentence: osteoclasts remove old bone and osteoblasts replace it; when breakdown outpaces building for years, bone becomes porous (osteoporosis). Low blood calcium → PTH → more osteoclast activity.

Exam Strategy

How to work a bone ID station

1
Whole bone? Name the shape first
Check proportions: shaft with two ends = long; cube = short; plate = flat; odd processes = irregular; seed in a tendon = sesamoid.
2
Sectioned model? Find the region
Is the pin on an end (epiphysis), the flare (metaphysis), or the shaft (diaphysis)? Then decide if it is on the surface (periosteum, articular cartilage), in the wall (compact bone), or inside (spongy bone, medullary cavity, endosteum).
3
Osteon diagram? Go from the center out
Central canal → concentric lamellae → lacunae with osteocytes → canaliculi. Then look between osteons (interstitial lamellae), around the edge (circumferential lamellae), and for crosswise channels (perforating canals).
Look-alikesHow to tell them apart
Epiphyseal plate vs. linePlate = cartilage band in a child (still growing). Line = thin bony line in an adult (done growing).
Periosteum vs. endosteumPeri- = around (outer surface). Endo- = within (lines the marrow cavity and trabeculae).
Central vs. perforating canalCentral runs lengthwise in the middle of an osteon; perforating runs crosswise and connects central canals.
Lacuna vs. osteocyteLacuna = the space. Osteocyte = the cell living in it. On an empty ground-bone slide you see lacunae; the cells are gone.
Concentric vs. interstitial vs. circumferential lamellaeConcentric = complete rings of one osteon. Interstitial = broken fragments between osteons. Circumferential = run around the entire bone.
Compact vs. spongy boneCompact = solid, with osteons; spongy = open lattice of trabeculae, no osteons, red marrow in the spaces.
Red vs. yellow marrowRed = blood-forming, in spongy bone. Yellow = mostly fat, in the adult medullary cavity.

Check Yourself

Bone identification quiz

Two tries per question; the explanation appears after you answer.

Question 1
Classify this bone by shape.
Why: This is the patella, which forms inside the quadriceps tendon, so it is a sesamoid bone. (Courses that use only four shape classes sometimes call it short.)
Question 2
Classify this bone by shape.
Why: A vertebra, with a body plus processes pointing in several directions, fits no simple shape class, so it is irregular.
Question 3
A phalanx of the finger is classified as a...
Why: It has a shaft and two ends and is longer than it is wide. Shape class depends on proportions, not size.
Question 4
Which bones are short bones?
Why: Carpals (wrist) and tarsals (ankle) are cube-like short bones. Metacarpals and metatarsals have shafts, so they are long bones.
Question 5
On a sectioned adult long bone, the thin bony line crossing each end is the...
Why: The epiphyseal plate (cartilage) ossifies after growth ends and leaves the epiphyseal line.
Question 6
Which membrane lines the medullary cavity?
Why: Endo- means within. The periosteum covers the outer surface; perichondrium surrounds cartilage, not bone.
Question 7
Which structure carries blood vessels lengthwise through the center of an osteon?
Why: Central canals run parallel to the long axis. Perforating canals run crosswise and connect them.
Question 8
Tiny channels that connect neighboring lacunae are...
Why: Canaliculi let osteocytes pass nutrients and signals to one another and to the central canal.
Question 9
Ring fragments filling the spaces between osteons are...
Why: They are remnants of older osteons that were partly broken down during remodeling.
Question 10
Where is red marrow found in an adult long bone?
Why: Adult medullary cavities hold yellow marrow. Red marrow persists in spongy bone, especially in the proximal epiphyses of the femur and humerus and in flat and irregular bones.
Question 11
A large, multinucleated cell sits in a pit on the bone surface. It is a(n)...
Why: Osteoclasts are giant cells with many nuclei and a ruffled border; they dissolve matrix.
Question 12
Which structure anchors tendons and ligaments to bone and contains cells for repair?
Why: Collagen (perforating) fibers tie the periosteum to the bone, and its inner layer holds osteogenic cells and osteoblasts.

Watch Out

Where students lose points on this lab

The mistakeThe fix
Calling a small bone "short."Shape is proportions. Phalanges, metacarpals, and metatarsals are long bones.
Writing "epiphyseal plate" on an adult bone.Adults have an epiphyseal line. The plate (cartilage) exists only while the bone is still growing.
Swapping periosteum and endosteum.Peri- = around the outside. Endo- = inside, lining the cavity.
Calling the medullary cavity "spongy bone."The cavity is the hollow center of the shaft (yellow marrow). Spongy bone is the lattice in the ends.
Mixing up central and perforating canals.Central = lengthwise, center of an osteon. Perforating = crosswise connector.
Naming the cell when the pin is on the space.Pin on the hollow space = lacuna. Pin on the cell or its nucleus = osteocyte.
Forgetting interstitial lamellae.If the pin is on broken ring fragments between osteons, it is interstitial, not concentric.
Spelling slips: "Haversion," "canaliculus" for many, "lacunas."Haversian canal; canaliculi (plural); lacuna (one) / lacunae (many); lamella / lamellae.
